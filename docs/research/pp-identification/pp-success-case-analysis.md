# Personal Project Success Case Analysis

## a) Scope and Evidence Limits

- Dataset: 64 records in [success-cases.json](./pp-success-case/success-cases.json)
- Evidence status: all 64 cases are showcase-only
- Public IB scores: none
- Supported conclusions: completion patterns, documented practices, and visible project characteristics
- Unsupported conclusion: showcase selection proves a high grade
- Limitation: selected showcase cases reveal association, not causation

## b) Source-Supported Findings

### Recurring Factors

1. Specific learning goal paired with a concrete product: all 64 records
2. Finished, public artifact: exhibition, deployment, demonstration, release, or repository
3. Clear audience or problem: peers, schools, community groups, technical users, or people with accessibility and health needs
4. Staged execution: milestones, calendars, learning phases, or subsystem-based plans
5. Measurable verification: tests, usage figures, operating logs, calibration data, or criteria-based evaluation
6. Adaptation to constraints: deployment, dependency changes, weak documentation, sensor noise, component compatibility, or mechanical tolerance

### Strong Practices

1. Planning:
   - Explicit success criteria or rubrics: cases 22, 33, 34, 35, 37, 39, 46, and 55
   - Staged plans or milestones: cases 26, 33, 38, 39, 40, 41, and 47
   - Modular hardware planning: cases 4, 16, 29, 47, and 48
2. Documentation:
   - GitHub-linked evidence: at least 18 cases
   - Theory and iteration process journal: case 7
   - Timestamped commit history: case 20
   - Investigation or operational logs: cases 40, 45, and 61
3. Development:
   - Small feature or subsystem stages
   - Iteration in response to technical failures
   - Deployment or complete packaging rather than code alone
   - Real constraints incorporated into design decisions
4. Testing:
   - Controlled computer-vision trials: case 14
   - Before-and-after grip-force measurement: case 17
   - Public player testing: case 43
   - Seven consecutive error-free operating days: case 45
   - Stress, usability, observational, or calibration testing: cases 47, 48, 52, 56, and 59
5. Reflection:
   - Product comparison against defined criteria
   - Explanation of problems, adaptations, and resulting learning
   - Explicit rubric-based self-evaluation: case 44
   - Reflection evidence is less visible in the dataset than planning and product creation

## c) Common Project Types and Scopes; Inference

Dominant-product classification:

1. Software or digital products: 45 cases
2. Hardware or hardware-software systems: 18 cases
3. Standalone event: 1 case

Overlapping themes:

1. Games and simulations: approximately 16 cases
2. Web products: approximately 14 cases
3. Mobile applications: 8 cases
4. AI or machine-learning-labelled projects: 8 cases

Typical scope:

> one usable artifact focused on a bounded user problem or learning challenge

More ambitious projects divided work into subsystems such as interface, data, model, deployment, mechanics, electronics, and firmware.

## d) Field-Relevant Patterns

### Software Engineering

1. Version control and timestamped progress evidence
2. Modular feature development
3. Deployment, packaging, and public usability
4. Dependency and API-change management
5. Accessibility, privacy, uptime, and error-handling criteria
6. Testing against defined functional and user-experience requirements

### Machine Learning and AI

1. Narrow, testable application rather than a broad claim of intelligence
2. Documented theory, experiments, training iterations, and logs
3. Quantitative accuracy or behavioral evaluation
4. Clear state, reward, input, and failure definitions where applicable
5. Distinction between building a model and building an AI educational resource or API-based assistant

### School and Club Organization

1. Demonstrated community need
2. Early stakeholder involvement
3. Logistics, venue, partnership, and contingency planning
4. Participation, reach, adoption, or funds raised as evidence
5. Limited basis: only one case is a pure event project; several others combine organization with software

## e) Ranked Practical Lessons

1. Define detailed, measurable success criteria before creation.
2. Limit the core scope to one complete and testable product.
3. Preserve dated evidence throughout the process: plans, decisions, commits, screenshots, tests, failures, feedback, and revisions.
4. Evaluate every success criterion with objective evidence.
5. Divide work into research, prototype, implementation, integration, testing, and refinement stages.
6. Involve real users or stakeholders before final development and during evaluation.
7. Explain changes, evidence-based decisions, unresolved limitations, and learning impact.
8. Deploy, publish, package, or demonstrate a usable final result.
9. Treat showcase selection as completion evidence only, never as grade evidence.

## f) Analytical Inference

The strongest overall pattern is not technical complexity. It is alignment between a focused learning goal, a feasible product, measurable criteria, continuous evidence, iterative testing, and explicit reflection.
