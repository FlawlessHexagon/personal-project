# Success Criteria Formats Analysis

> AI-driven research: compiled and analyzed with AI assistance. Source-supported findings and AI-generated interpretations are distinguished in the research; claims should be checked against the cited sources.

## a) Scope and Evidence Limits

- Dataset: 10 records in [success-criteria-formats.json](ai-research-success-criteria-formats.json).
- Research date: 2026-10-06.
- Evidence: primary-source guidance from educational, software, engineering, and user-experience organizations.
- The set includes criteria formats and related evaluation, evidence organization, goal-setting, and prioritization methods. These are not ten interchangeable or mutually exclusive formats.
- Source descriptions are distinguished from project-specific analysis and authored examples in each report.
- No source establishes that a particular format guarantees a high IB grade.
- This is a selected comparison, not an exhaustive survey or empirical ranking.
- A hybrid format and final product criteria remain decisions for Phase 02 Steps 2 and 3.

## c) Comparative Analysis

### Describing Success

- Acceptance conditions fit observable functional requirements.
- Scenarios clarify behavior under particular circumstances, including missing information and failure cases.
- Rubric descriptions capture degrees of quality, but require distinct and defensible descriptions.

### Gathering Evidence

- Usability metrics assess whether people can complete meaningful tasks.
- Benchmarking assesses improvement relative to a defined reference point.
- Reliability targets require repeated observations with explicit conditions and denominators.
- A verification matrix connects each requirement to its source and evaluation approach.

### Supporting Scope and Delivery

- OKRs can connect measures to purpose, but aspirational outcomes need care when used as acceptance conditions.
- A Definition of Done supports delivery readiness; it does not replace product-specific evaluation.
- MoSCoW expresses priority; it does not establish a passing condition.

These judgments are analysis of suitability, not comparative effectiveness demonstrated by the sources.

## d) Project-Relevant Patterns; Inference

1. Note operations and persistence suit acceptance conditions and scenarios.
2. Grounded AI responses need explicit quality definitions and repeated evaluation across a predefined set of questions and notes.
3. On-device operation needs technical evidence; users' understanding of privacy needs separate user evidence. Neither alone establishes complete security or user trust.
4. Reduced organization effort needs task-based evidence; feature availability alone does not establish that benefit.
5. Response performance needs a specified phone, workload, timing definition, and repeated trials.
6. Proactive assistance needs both relevant-use scenarios and cases where intervention should not occur.
7. Long-term habit formation would require longitudinal evidence; a short usability test cannot establish that the app creates a lasting habit.

## e) Ranked Practical Lessons

1. Describe observable success before deciding how to score it.
2. Pair every criterion with obtainable evidence and explicit evaluation conditions.
3. Justify thresholds through user needs, research, or early device experiments.
4. Keep mandatory conditions separate from optional improvements.
5. Retain actual measurements and limitations alongside any pass/fail verdict.
6. Distinguish functional correctness, repeated reliability, usability, and user benefit.
7. Use simple identifiers to connect criteria, tests, and later reflection.
8. Avoid borrowing the administrative complexity of larger engineering frameworks.

## f) Implications for Phase 02 Step 2

Candidate elements for a hybrid format are requirement identifiers, purpose or audience justification, observable acceptance conditions, evaluation methods, and recorded evidence. Scenarios, numerical targets, quality descriptions, and priorities may be added where useful.

This is a shortlist for the next design discussion, not an established final format.
